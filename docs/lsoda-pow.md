end state), the three benchmark cases and the banded diffusion problem

none for the benchmark cases). Where the counts differ it is a knife-edge of
the step selection; the error moves with the steps taken, not with the power

Worst excess over SciPy's nfev: math.Pow +12.9 %, powRecip +5.2 %. Median error (tolerances): math.Pow 18.8, powRecip 18.8; largest 6.39e+07 and 6.39e+07.

Estimated total time with a 10 us right-hand side: math.Pow 467.1 ms, powRecip 463.0 ms.

**Decision.** The sum of the counts decides: `powRecip` takes 45 752 evaluations,
0.3 % under SciPy's 45 907, `math.Pow` 46 195, 0.6 % over; the worst case is 5.2 %
over with `powRecip` and 12.9 % with `math.Pow`; njev and the error are the same
(median 18.8 tolerances for both). `math.Pow` runs the corpus 6.7 % faster with
the right-hand side as it is (50 ns), `powRecip` saves 443 evaluations: they pay
for its 343 us as soon as the right-hand side costs 0.8 us, so at 1 us `powRecip`
is 0.2 % faster and at 10 us 0.9 %. `powRecip` stays the default, and `Exact`
keeps the independent `ddPow`. The default is therefore held to SciPy's counts by
sum (at most 3 % over on any architecture, `TestLsodaCorpusCounts`; 0.34 % under
on arm64, 0.63 % under on amd64) and case by case to at most 6 % over (the worst
case measured here is 5.2 % on arm64, 10 % on amd64 where the corpus bound is
20 %), no longer by count equality; `Exact` keeps equality.
